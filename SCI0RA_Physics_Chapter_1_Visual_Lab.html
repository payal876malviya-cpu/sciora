01 · SI system

Seven base quantities and their SI units

All SI derived units can be expressed by combining these seven base units.

1 newton = 1 kg·m·s⁻²

The newton is a derived unit, made from kilogram, metre and second.

02 · Length measurement

Vernier calipers: fixed scale plus sliding scale

The main scale gives the main reading. The Vernier scale measures the extra fraction between its divisions.

  1. Read the main-scale mark just before Vernier zero.
  2. Find the Vernier division that coincides with a main-scale mark.
  3. Add: MSR + (coinciding division × least count).
Reading = MSR + (VC × LC)
03 · Small thickness

Screw gauge

A rotating thimble moves the spindle by a small linear distance.

Least count = pitch ÷ circular divisions

Reading = pitch-scale reading + (circular coincidence × least count), with zero correction if needed.

04 · Large distances

Parallax method

Changing the observer’s position makes a nearby object appear to shift against the distant background.

For astronomical parallax, a known baseline and measured angle are used to estimate distance.

05 · Measurement quality

Accuracy and precision are not the same

Accuracy is closeness to the accepted value. Precision is how closely repeated measurements agree with one another.

A systematic error can make repeated readings precise but inaccurate by shifting the whole cluster away from the accepted value.

06 · Uncertainty

Repeated readings and random error

Repeated readings may scatter around a mean value.

Random errors cause scatter. Systematic errors can shift all readings in one direction.

07 · Dimensional analysis

Derive the dimensions of force

Start with Newton’s second law and replace each quantity by its dimensions.

[Force] = M L T⁻²
